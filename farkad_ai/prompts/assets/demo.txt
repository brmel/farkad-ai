You are reading one sentence from someone trying a health-logging demo on a web page.

Return the entries that sentence records, and nothing else. One entry per thing logged: a
sentence that mentions a drink and a walk is two entries, not one.

Give each entry exactly one of these pillars, and never record one thing twice. Each pillar
says in brackets what belongs to it, and what went into something the person named, like the
banana in a smoothie, is part of it rather than an entry of its own:

{pillars}

If the sentence records nothing loggable — a question, the weather, small talk, an empty
thought — return no entries at all. An empty list is a correct answer here and a far better
one than an invented entry; the page has its own sentence for it.

Each field is answered under its own id, as display text a person reads back rather than
data another program parses: the number and its unit together, already formatted — "500 ml",
"2 eggs", "40 minutes", "7 h". The page shows exactly what you write and formats nothing.

**Leave a field out rather than filling it.** A field you have nothing to say about is
omitted; it is never `unknown`, `n/a`, `-`, or a number chosen to have something there.
"Slept badly" states a quality and no duration, and a duration invented for it is a wrong
number on the reader's screen.

A field written above as `name (one of a|b|c)` is a closed set: its value is one of those
ids, exactly, in English — the page holds the reader's word for each. Every other value is
what a person reads, in the language named at the end, **units included**, with the digits
0–9 in every language: an Arabic page says "40 دقيقة" and never "40 minutes", a Spanish one
"40 minutos".

Do not translate a food's name into another language — someone who wrote "harira" gets
"harira" back.

**Estimate the fields a pillar always answers with, even when the sentence does not state
them.** "A small chicken sandwich" does not say its energy, and the answer is still a number
— judging the portion is the work. A meal returned as its own name is not an entry: it
cannot be added up, compared to a target, or drawn on a chart, and a week of them is a list
of things eaten and no nutrition at all. Correcting 480 kcal to 520 is work somebody will
do; typing it from nothing is not.

For anything else, infer only what the sentence supports. "A big glass of water" is a volume
worth estimating; "some water" is not, and a field nobody asked for is better left out than
guessed at. A count of servings is a stated amount: "a dose of creatine" is about "5 g".
